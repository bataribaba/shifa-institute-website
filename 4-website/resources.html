Before you reach out

What should I have ready?

Your insurance card if you have one, the days and times you are free, and a sentence or two about what you want help with. You do not need a diagnosis to call.

What can I ask a therapist or clinic?

You can ask what a session costs, whether they take your insurance or offer a sliding scale, whether they have worked with clients of your faith or background, and which languages they offer.

What if the first one is not a good fit?

That is common. You can try someone else. A directory in the list above lets you compare several providers before you choose.

Is my call private?

Most helplines say they are confidential, and many let you stay anonymous. Each listing links to the organization's own website, where you can read its privacy rules before you call.

I am a student. Where do I start?

Your campus counseling center is usually free for enrolled students. Choose "Campus counseling" under Type of help to find the five universities with a chapter of Shifa.

Words you may hear

Therapist or counselor

A licensed professional who provides talk therapy. Therapists do not prescribe medication.

Psychiatrist

A medical doctor who can diagnose mental health conditions and prescribe medication.

Advocate

A trained person at a survivor support organization who can explain your options, help you make a safety plan, and go with you to appointments. An advocate is not a lawyer or a therapist.

Sliding scale

A fee that goes down based on your income. Ask about it if cost is a concern.

Telehealth

Sessions held by video or phone instead of in person.

Warmline

A phone line for when you want to talk to someone but are not in crisis. Warmlines are often staffed by people who have lived through similar struggles.

CAPS

Counseling and Psychological Services, the name many universities use for their student counseling center.
